Module 1

Density and Derived Properties

Fundamental Definitions

  • • Density ($\rho$): Mass per unit volume ($kg/m^3$).
  • • Specific Volume ($v$): It is the inverse of density ($v=1/\rho$). It indicates how much volume 1 kg of mass occupies.
  • • Specific Weight ($\gamma$): Force of gravity (weight) per unit volume ($\gamma = \rho g$). It is the key property in Hydrostatics.
  • • Specific Gravity ($SG$): Dimensionless comparative ratio: $SG = \rho_{fluid} / \rho_{water@4^\circ C}$.

Industrial Applications

These properties govern the design of gravity separators (sedimentation, centrifugation). In the oil sector, the standard is the API gravity, while in food, Brix degrees are used.

$$ v = 1/\rho $$
$$ \gamma = \rho g $$
$$ SG = \rho / \rho_{water} $$
$$ API = \frac{141.5}{SG} - 131.5 $$

EXAMPLE 1 Commercial Classification of Crude Oil
▼

Problem Statement:

A production engineer in an oil field takes a sample of crude oil and measures its specific gravity, obtaining a value of $SG = 0.85$. It is required to calculate its API degrees to classify it commercially and determine its price in the international market. (Remember that crude is considered "light" if its API > 31.1).

Calculation:

$$ API = \frac{141.5}{0.85} - 131.5 \approx 35^\circ $$

Conclusion: Since $35 > 31.1$, it is classified as high-value LIGHT CRUDE.

Module 2

Compressibility

Compressibility measures how much a fluid's volume decreases when pressure increases. It is quantified by the Bulk Modulus of Elasticity ($E_v$). A high $E_v$ indicates great rigidity.

Typical values at 20°C:

  • Mercury: 28.5 GPa (Extremely incompressible)
  • Water: 2.2 GPa (Reference for liquids)
  • Ethanol: 1.15 GPa (More compressible than water)
Incompressibility Criterion in Gases:
$$ Ma = \frac{V}{c} < 0.3 $$
EXAMPLE 2Commercial Flight Aerodynamics
▼

A commercial airplane flies at a cruising speed of 150 m/s. At cruising altitude, the air temperature is low and the speed of sound is reduced to 340 m/s. Can the aerodynamic engineer use the simplified incompressible flow equations (simple Bernoulli) to model the air around the wings?

$$ Ma = \frac{150}{340} \approx 0.44 $$
Conclusion: Since $0.44 > 0.3$, the flow is COMPRESSIBLE.
Using incompressible equations would generate serious errors (>5%) in calculating lift.

Module 3

Absolute Viscosity

It is the measure of the fluid's "internal friction". In liquids, viscosity decreases drastically as temperature increases, which is critical when selecting motor oils. A golden rule in pumping systems is:

Energy Relationship
$$ Power_{pump} \propto \mu $$

The higher the viscosity, the higher the electrical energy consumption of the pump.

EXAMPLE 4Shear Stress Calculation
▼

A steel shaft moves linearly at a speed of 2 m/s inside a sleeve. The clearance between the shaft and the sleeve is filled with an oil film 0.5 mm thick and a dynamic viscosity of 0.05 Pa·s. Calculate the shear stress ($\tau$) opposing the movement.

Velocity gradient: $du/dy = 2 / 0.0005 = 4000 \, s^{-1}$.

$$ \tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy} = 0.05 \times 4000 = 200 \, Pa $$

Module 4

Capillarity

Phenomenon of the rise or fall of liquids in thin tubes, caused by surface tension ($\sigma$).

Petroleum Engineering Application: This concept is vital for understanding hydrocarbon recovery. Oil is trapped in the microscopic pores of sedimentary rocks and moves driven by capillary pressure, displacing connate water.

$$ h = \frac{2 \sigma \cos \theta}{\gamma r} $$
EXAMPLE 5Reading Error in Manometer
▼

To measure the level of a water tank, an external glass tube (piezometer) of 1 mm diameter is installed. Due to capillarity, the water rises through the glass beyond the actual liquid level inside the tank, falsifying the measurement. Calculate the magnitude of this reading error (extra height). (Data: $\sigma=0.073 N/m$).

Radius $r = 0.5 mm = 0.0005 m$.

$$ h = \frac{2(0.073)(1)}{9790(0.0005)} \approx 0.0298 \, m $$

Error: The instrument reads +2.98 cm above the actual level.

Module 5

Vapor Pressure

It is the pressure at which a liquid boils at a given temperature. Classification:

  • Volatile Liquids: Have high $P_v$ (e.g., Gasoline, Acetone). They evaporate easily.
  • Non-Volatile Liquids: Have low $P_v$ (e.g., Mercury, Oils).

Critical Risk: Cavitation

If the suction pressure drops below the vapor pressure ($P < P_v$), the liquid boils cold. The formed bubbles collapse violently upon entering the impeller, tearing off metallic material (pitting).

EXAMPLE 6Pump Operation Diagnosis
▼

A centrifugal pump is operating with hot water at 60°C in an industrial process. The operator notices that the vacuum gauge installed on the suction flange reads an absolute pressure of 15 kPa. Is there an imminent risk of cavitation damage?

Step 1: Consult the saturation curve. For $T=60^\circ C \rightarrow P_v \approx 19.9 \, kPa$.

$$ P_{suction} (15 \, kPa) < P_{vapor} (19.9 \, kPa) $$

YES, THERE IS A SEVERE RISK OF CAVITATION!

The water is boiling at the inlet. Suction pressure must be increased immediately.
